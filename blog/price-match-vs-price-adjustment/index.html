Price Match vs. Price Adjustment vs. Price Protection: What's the Difference?

Three terms that sound alike and work very differently. Asking for the wrong one is one of the easiest ways to get a no. Here's how to tell them apart.

Price Match Paradise Team Updated September 22, 2026 6 min read
Quick answer

A price match is when a store lowers its price to match a competitor, usually at checkout. A price adjustment is when a store refunds the difference after its own price drops, within a set window. Price protection is a credit card benefit that refunds a later price drop, but most major issuers have discontinued it.

Price match

You show the store a lower price from a competitor for the identical item, and it matches it. Fewer stores do this than before. Target stopped in July 2025, and Walmart only matches its own website. See the complete price matching guide.

Price adjustment

You already bought it, the same store lowered its price, and you ask for the difference back. Windows vary: Target 14 days, Costco 30 days, Best Buy 15 days for most products. See stores that refund price drops.

Credit card price protection

Some credit cards used to refund you if an item you bought dropped in price elsewhere. Most major issuers have dropped the benefit. Chase, Citi and Discover no longer offer it, according to card-benefit trackers. A few Capital One and some Visa and Mastercard cards may still include it. Check your card's benefits guide before counting on it.

Which one should you ask for?

  • Haven't paid yet and a competitor is cheaper? Ask for a price match, if the store offers one. See what to say.
  • Already bought it and the same store dropped its price? Ask for a price adjustment.
  • Already bought it and another store is now cheaper? Check your card's benefits, and whether the store will match after purchase (a few do within the return window).

FAQ

What is the difference between a price match and a price adjustment?

A price match lowers the price to match a competitor, usually at checkout. A price adjustment refunds the difference when the same store lowers its own price after you buy.

What is credit card price protection?

A card benefit that refunds the difference if an item you bought drops in price within a set period. Most major issuers, including Chase, Citi and Discover, have discontinued it.

Which stores offer price adjustments?

Target (14 days), Costco (30 days), Best Buy (15 days for most products) and Home Depot (typically 30 days). Walmart doesn't.

Can I get a price match after I bought something?

Sometimes. A few stores will match a competitor within the return window, but most post-purchase requests are handled as price adjustments on the store's own price.
