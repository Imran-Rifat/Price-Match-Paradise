How to Compare Prices by Scanning a Barcode

The barcode on every product is the most accurate way to compare prices between stores. Here's how it works and how to do it on your phone in seconds.

Why scanning beats searching by name

Search "paper towels 12 pack" and you'll get dozens of similar products in different sizes, sheet counts and brands. Comparing them is slow and error-prone. A barcode avoids that problem: it points to one specific product. The same brand, flavor and size carries the same barcode at every store that sells it.

That's also why barcodes are ideal for price matching. Stores only match identical items, and "same barcode" is the clearest proof that two items are identical.

How product barcodes work (UPC vs. EAN)

TypeDigitsWhere you'll see it
UPC-A12Most products in the US and Canada
EAN-1313Products from most other countries, and many imports
UPC-E8 (compressed)Small packages like gum, cans and cosmetics
QR codeVariesLinks, coupons and menus. Usually not a product ID

The first digits of a UPC identify the company that makes the product (assigned by GS1, the global barcode standards body). The next digits identify the specific item, and the last digit is a check digit that catches scanning errors. A UPC-A code can also be written as a 13-digit EAN with a leading zero, so good scanners treat them as the same product.

Tips for accurate scans

  • Scan the product, not the shelf label. Shelf tags often carry the store's internal code, not the product's UPC.
  • Hold steady in good light, about 4 to 8 inches away, with the whole barcode in frame. Curved cans scan better when you tilt them slightly.
  • Produce, deli and meat are different. Loose produce uses 4 or 5-digit PLU codes, and weighed items get a store-printed barcode with the price built in. These won't compare across stores, but packaged versions will.
  • Watch for multipacks. A 6-pack and a single can have different barcodes. Scan the one you're actually buying.
  • Check the unit price. When a store sells a different size, compare price per ounce or per count.

FAQ

Can I compare prices by scanning a barcode with my phone?

Yes. A barcode price comparison app uses your phone's camera to read the UPC or EAN code and looks up the same product at other stores. No extra hardware needed.

Is a UPC the same at every store?

For the same manufactured product, yes. The UPC identifies the brand, product and size, so it's the same wherever the item is sold. Store-brand products and store-printed labels for weighed items are the exceptions.

What is the difference between UPC and EAN?

UPC-A has 12 digits and is used mainly in the US and Canada. EAN-13 has 13 digits and is used in most other countries. A UPC can be written as an EAN by adding a leading zero.

Why won't a barcode scan find my product?

The product may be new, regional or a store brand, or you may have scanned a shelf tag or a store-printed label instead of the product's own barcode. Try the barcode printed on the package itself.

Can I use a barcode scan as proof for a price match?

A scan helps you find the identical item and its current price. Stores usually want to see the competitor's current price for that exact item, and a comparison screen or the competitor's live listing on your phone generally works.
