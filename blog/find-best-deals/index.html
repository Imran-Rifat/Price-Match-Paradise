How to Find the Best Deals Every Week (Without Spending Hours)

Real deal hunters don't browse all day. They follow a short routine, buy on the sale cycle and check prices before they pay. Here's the system.

Price Match Paradise Team Updated September 22, 2026 10 min read
Quick answer

To find the best deals every week, spend 15 minutes with your stores' weekly ads and apps, clip digital coupons, plan purchases around sales, stock up when staples hit their low price, and compare the sale price with other stores before you buy. Then use price-drop refunds where your store offers them.

The 15-minute weekly deal routine

  1. Check the weekly ads for the 2–3 stores you actually shop at. New ads often start midweek or on Sunday, depending on the chain (best day to shop). Most are in the store's app.
  2. Clip digital coupons in each store's app for things already on your list. They apply automatically at checkout.
  3. Build your list around the sales, especially meat, produce and the household items you use most.
  4. Note stock-up prices for staples that keep: pantry goods, paper products, cleaning supplies, frozen food.
  5. In the store, scan before you buy. Sale prices aren't always the lowest prices, and a quick scan shows whether another store is cheaper.

Where the real deals are

Store apps and loyalty programs

Many chains now put their best prices behind a free app or loyalty account: digital coupons, member prices and personalized offers. If you shop somewhere weekly, sign up.

Weekly ads and "loss leaders"

Stores advertise a few items at very low prices to get you in the door, often on the front page of the ad. Buy those, and stay disciplined about the rest of the cart.

Markdowns and clearance

Look for markdown stickers on meat nearing its sell-by date (cook or freeze it that day), bakery items, seasonal goods after a holiday, and discontinued packaging.

Store brands on sale

A store brand that's already cheaper, on sale, with a digital coupon, is often the lowest price in the whole store.

Buy on the sale cycle, not when you run out

Many grocery and household items go on sale in a repeating pattern, often every several weeks. If you only buy when you run out, you'll often pay full price. If you buy enough at the low point to last until the next sale, you almost never will.

A simple way to learn the cycle: for the 10–15 items you buy most, note the price each time you see a sale. Price-drop alerts in an app do this for you.

FAQ

What day do grocery stores release new deals?

It depends on the chain. Many weekly ads start midweek or on Sunday. Check your store's app or website, where the new ad usually appears first.

Are sale prices always the lowest price?

No. A sale only means the price dropped at that store. Another store may still be cheaper, so compare before you buy.

How do I know when to stock up?

Track the sale price of the items you buy most. When one hits its usual low, buy enough to last until the next sale, as long as it keeps and you have space.

Are digital coupons worth it?

Yes, for items already on your list. They take a minute to clip in the store app and apply automatically at checkout.

What is the best time of year to buy electronics?

Late November around Black Friday and Cyber Monday is typically strong, and TVs often drop in early spring ahead of new models. Always compare prices across stores, because not every "deal" is the lowest price.
