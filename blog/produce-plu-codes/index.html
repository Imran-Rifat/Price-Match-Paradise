Produce PLU Codes Explained: What the Numbers on Fruit Stickers Mean

That little sticker on your banana is a price code the cashier uses to ring it up. Here's how to read it, how to spot organic, and how to make sure you're charged correctly.

Price Match Paradise Team Updated September 22, 2026 6 min read
Quick answer

PLU (price look-up) codes are the 4- or 5-digit numbers on produce stickers. A 4-digit code in the 3000s or 4000s is conventionally grown produce. A 5-digit code starting with 9 is organic (organic bananas are 94011, regular bananas 4011). A code starting with 8 does not mean GMO. That prefix is being used to add new codes.

How to read a PLU code

CodeWhat it means
4 digits, 3000–4999
e.g. 4011		Conventionally grown produce
5 digits starting with 9
e.g. 94011		Organic version of the same item
5 digits starting with 8
83000–84999		New item codes. Not an indicator of GMO produce

PLU codes are assigned by the International Federation for Produce Standards (IFPS) and identify the commodity, variety and often the size. The same code is used by stores around the world, which is why cashiers can key them in quickly.

The "8 means GMO" myth

You may have read that a code starting with 8 means genetically modified. According to IFPS, the 8 prefix was once reserved for that purpose but was never used at retail. It's now being used to create more codes as the 3000 and 4000 series fill up. PLU codes don't tell you whether produce is genetically modified.

How PLU codes lead to overcharges

  • Organic rung up for conventional. Keying 94011 instead of 4011 charges the organic price. Check produce lines on your receipt.
  • Wrong variety. Different apple or pepper varieties can have very different prices per pound.
  • Per-each vs. per-pound. Some items are priced each, others by weight. Check the shelf sign.
  • Self-checkout mistakes. Choosing the wrong item from the picture menu is easy. Type the PLU from the sticker instead.

PLU codes vs. barcodes

Loose produce uses PLU codes. Packaged produce (bagged salad, a 3 lb bag of apples, berries in clamshells) usually has a regular UPC barcode, just like other groceries. Items weighed in the store, like deli meat, get a store-printed barcode with the price built in.

That matters for comparing prices: barcoded packaged items can be compared across stores, while loose produce and store-weighed items can't be scanned for a comparison because their codes aren't product-specific. For those, compare the price per pound on the shelf sign.

Quick ways to save on produce

  • Buy in season, and frozen out of season.
  • Compare loose vs. bagged per pound. The bag is often cheaper, but not always.
  • Skip pre-cut unless the time savings are worth it to you.

FAQ

What does a PLU code starting with 9 mean?

It's the organic version of the item. The 9 is added in front of the 4-digit conventional code, so regular bananas are 4011 and organic bananas are 94011.

Does a PLU code starting with 8 mean GMO?

No. According to IFPS, the 8 prefix was reserved for that purpose but never used at retail. It's now used to create new codes.

Who assigns PLU codes?

The International Federation for Produce Standards (IFPS).

Can I compare produce prices with a barcode scanner?

Packaged produce with a UPC barcode, yes. Loose produce with a PLU sticker and store-weighed items can't be compared by scanning. Compare the price per pound instead.
