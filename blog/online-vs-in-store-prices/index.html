Online vs. In-Store Prices: Why They're Different (and How to Get the Lower One)

The same store, the same item, two different prices. Here's why online and in-store prices differ, which stores will give you the lower one, and how to check in seconds.

Price Match Paradise Team Updated September 22, 2026 6 min read
Quick answer

Online and in-store prices differ because stores price by channel and location: online prices compete nationally, while store prices reflect local costs and competition, and some online prices include shipping. The good news: Walmart matches Walmart.com prices in its US stores and Target matches Target.com, so you can ask for the lower online price at checkout.

Why online and store prices differ

  • Different competition. Online prices compete with every website. Store prices compete with the stores nearby.
  • Different costs. Stores pay for rent, staff and shelf space. Online orders have shipping and fulfillment costs.
  • Location pricing. Many chains set store prices by area. See how grocery pricing works.
  • Online-only deals and fees. Some deals exist only online, and some online prices include shipping and handling.

FAQ

Why are prices different online and in store?

Stores price online and in-store channels separately, based on different competition, costs and location. Some online prices also include shipping.

Does Walmart match its online price in store?

Yes. Walmart US stores match the Walmart.com price for identical items sold by Walmart and in stock online, if you ask at checkout. Rollbacks, clearance and special events are excluded.

Does Target match Target.com prices in store?

Yes. Target matches its own lower prices from Target.com and the Target app, at checkout or within 14 days of purchase.

Are Costco online prices the same as in the warehouse?

Not always. Costco.com prices can differ and may include shipping and handling, and the two channels are treated separately for price adjustments.
