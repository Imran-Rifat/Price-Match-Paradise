20 Cheap Healthy Groceries to Build Budget Meals Around

Eating well on a budget is mostly about knowing which foods give you the most for your money. These 20 staples are inexpensive, versatile and easy to build meals around.

Price Match Paradise Team Updated September 22, 2026 7 min read
Quick answer

Some of the cheapest healthy groceries are eggs, dried or canned beans, lentils, oats, brown rice, frozen vegetables, cabbage, carrots, potatoes, bananas, canned tomatoes, peanut butter, plain yogurt, canned fish and whole chickens. Buy them in store brands, stock up when they're on sale, and compare prices across stores.

Proteins

  1. Eggs: breakfast, fried rice, frittatas, baking.
  2. Dried or canned beans: chili, burritos, soups, salads. Dried is usually cheaper per serving.
  3. Lentils: cook fast without soaking. Great in soups and curries.
  4. Whole chicken: often cheaper per pound than parts, and it stretches across several meals.
  5. Canned tuna, salmon or sardines: shelf-stable protein for sandwiches and pasta.
  6. Peanut butter: an inexpensive pantry staple.
  7. Plain yogurt in large tubs: breakfast, sauces, marinades.

Grains and starches

  1. Oats: breakfast, baking, even savory bowls.
  2. Brown or white rice: a base for countless meals. Buy big bags at a good unit price.
  3. Pasta, especially the store brand.
  4. Potatoes: filling, versatile and long-lasting.
  5. Sweet potatoes: roast, mash or bake.

Fruits and vegetables

  1. Frozen vegetables: picked and frozen quickly, no waste, often cheaper than fresh.
  2. Frozen fruit: smoothies, oatmeal, yogurt.
  3. Cabbage: lasts for weeks and works in slaws, stir-fries and soups.
  4. Carrots: cheap and long-lasting.
  5. Onions and garlic: the base of countless meals.
  6. Bananas: often one of the least expensive fruits.
  7. Apples in bags, especially in season.
  8. Canned tomatoes: sauces, soups, chili, curries.

FAQ

What are the cheapest healthy foods to buy?

Eggs, dried and canned beans, lentils, oats, rice, potatoes, frozen vegetables and fruit, cabbage, carrots, bananas, canned tomatoes, canned fish, peanut butter and whole chickens are among the most budget-friendly.

Are frozen vegetables as healthy as fresh?

Frozen vegetables are a convenient, nutritious option that won't spoil, and they're often cheaper than fresh out of season.

Is it cheaper to buy a whole chicken?

Often, yes, per pound, and one chicken can stretch across several meals, including soup from the bones.

How do I eat healthy on a tight budget?

Build meals around inexpensive staples, plan your week, buy store brands, stock up on sales and compare prices across stores.
