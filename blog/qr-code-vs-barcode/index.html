QR Code vs. Barcode: What's the Difference (and Which One to Scan)?

More packages now carry both a barcode and a QR code. They look different, store different things and do different jobs. Here's which one helps you save money.

Price Match Paradise Team Updated September 22, 2026 5 min read
Quick answer

A barcode (UPC or EAN) is a one-dimensional code that stores a short product number, and it's what registers and price comparison apps use to identify the exact product. A QR code is a two-dimensional square that can store much more, usually a web link. To compare prices, scan the barcode.

Why barcodes are best for price comparison

A product barcode identifies the exact product (brand, item and size), and the same number is used at every store that sells it. That's what makes it perfect for comparing prices. More in what the numbers on a barcode mean.

What QR codes on packaging do

QR codes on products usually link to a website: recipes, ingredients, recycling information, promotions or registration. Some newer product codes combine product identification with a link, and the industry is gradually adding 2D codes to packaging. Today, the standard product barcode is still the most reliable way to identify a product for price comparison.

Be careful with QR codes in public places. A QR code can send you to any website. Check the web address before entering any personal or payment information.

FAQ

What is the difference between a QR code and a barcode?

A barcode stores a short product number in vertical lines. A QR code is a square grid that can store much more information, usually a web link.

Can I compare prices by scanning a QR code?

Usually not. QR codes on packaging typically link to a website. Scan the product barcode (UPC/EAN) to compare prices.

Are QR codes replacing barcodes?

The retail industry is gradually adding 2D codes to packaging, but standard product barcodes are still used at checkout and for price lookup.

Are QR codes safe to scan?

Generally, but a QR code can link to any site. Check the web address before entering personal or payment details.
