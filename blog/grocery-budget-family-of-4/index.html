Grocery Budget for a Family of 4 in 2026: What's Normal, and How to Spend Less

Is your grocery bill normal? The USDA publishes four official food budgets. Here's what they say for a family of four, and a simple way to set yours and bring it down.

Price Match Paradise Team Updated September 22, 2026 8 min read
Quick answer

According to the USDA's food plans (December 2025 report), a family of four with two adults and two school-age children spends about $993 a month on the Thrifty plan, $1,094 on the Low-Cost plan, $1,347 on the Moderate plan and $1,628 on the Liberal plan, for groceries with all meals prepared at home. A realistic budget for most families falls somewhere in that range.

Reference family: two adults aged 19–50 and two children aged 6–8 and 9–11. December 2025 USDA Cost of Food report. USDA updates these figures regularly, so check the latest report for current numbers.

All four plans describe a nutritious diet. The difference is food choices, variety and how much time goes into planning and cooking. The Thrifty plan is also the basis for SNAP benefit amounts.

How to set your own budget

  1. Track 4 weeks of grocery spending. Receipts or your bank statement give you your real baseline.
  2. Compare with the USDA range. Families with teenagers, special diets or high-cost areas will naturally sit higher. Younger children, lower.
  3. Pick a target one step down from where you are now, not two. A sustainable 10% cut beats a crash budget.
  4. Set a weekly number and track it as you shop.

  5. Cook once, eat twice. Planned leftovers replace takeout.
  6. Cut food waste by shopping the fridge first and freezing what you won't use in time.

FAQ

How much should a family of 4 spend on groceries per month?

Under the USDA food plans (December 2025 report), about $993 (Thrifty) to $1,628 (Liberal) per month for two adults and two school-age children, with the Low-Cost plan at about $1,094 and the Moderate plan at about $1,347.

What is the USDA Thrifty Food Plan?

It's the lowest-cost of USDA's four food plans, describing a nutritious diet at minimal cost. It's used as the basis for SNAP benefits.

Do the USDA numbers include eating out?

No. They assume all meals and snacks are prepared at home.
